# Sources and provenance

This skill's initial taxonomy is based on the de-duplicated union discussed for two Chinese Telegram sticker packs (`guibianshu` and `FallacyCN`), while the definitions in this package are independently paraphrased for machine analysis rather than copied from the stickers.

## Primary / high-value references

### Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Fallacies
https://iep.utm.edu/fallacy/

Why it matters:
- Broad reference catalog of named fallacies.
- Explicitly distinguishes formal and informal fallacies.
- Emphasizes that a charge of fallacious reasoning itself requires justification.

### Purdue OWL — Logical Fallacies
https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/logic_in_argumentative_writing/fallacies.html

Why it matters:
- Practical definitions and examples for common fallacies.
- Useful for distinguishing argument structure from mere rhetoric.

### Information is Beautiful — Rhetological Fallacies
https://informationisbeautiful.net/visualizations/rhetological-fallacies/

Method/background:
https://informationisbeautiful.net/2012/rhetological-fallacies/

Public research sheet:
https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1XIPtgp4RtboC40SW6HFMJ6jGJPnkd_yGu0kZMVYYGTQ/edit

Why it matters:
- Source family for the Chinese “诡辩术” visual/sticker taxonomy.
- Intentionally mixes logical fallacies and rhetorical devices; therefore this skill marks some findings as `rhetorical` rather than pretending every item is a formal fallacy.

### Your Logical Fallacy Is / School of Thought
https://yourlogicalfallacyis.com/

Why it matters:
- Source family for the 24-item common-fallacy set associated with the other Chinese sticker pack.
- Particularly useful for plain-language distinctions such as straw man, false cause, fallacy fallacy, loaded question, Texas sharpshooter, and no-true-Scotsman.

## Important methodological caution

Named-fallacy lists are taxonomies, not substitutes for analysis. Different sources split, merge, or name related defects differently. Therefore:

1. diagnose the actual inference first;
2. choose the narrowest useful label second;
3. explain the logical defect in ordinary language;
4. never treat the label itself as proof.
